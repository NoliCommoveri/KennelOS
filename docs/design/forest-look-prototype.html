Design notes

Getting around

The tab bar (a left rail on desktop) is on every screen, so Home and every hub are one tap away. Every screen after the first has a back arrow that returns to the screen you came from, even across tabs, and back puts you where you were on that screen. Tapping a tab also opens it fresh and adds a step to back.

Search

A search button sits in the header of every screen (on desktop it's a box with a / shortcut; Ctrl+K or Cmd+K work too). It opens one keyword box over everything: screens, dogs, litters, waitlist families, contacts, sales, health and breeding events, money entries, every setup question and every report. Results are grouped by kind, with the matching words highlighted, and every word you type has to match. Recent searches stay at the top of the empty box. Opening a money entry or an event lands on its list already narrowed to it.

Reminders, Coming up and the calendar

One set of dated things feeds all three. Reminders (Home's "All reminders") groups what she set a reminder for into Overdue, Next 7 days and Later; tapping one opens Done, Snooze (tomorrow, 3 days, 1 week or a date) and a way to the dog or litter. Done ones stay on that dog's or litter's history and can be put back. Coming up is everything from today on, a week at a time, go-homes with their times. The Calendar is a month at a time: dots on a phone, names on desktop; tapping a day lists it, with Add to Google Calendar. Notifications (the bell) lists what families and the server did while she was away, new first; which ones she's told about stays in Setup › Notifications. The kennel name under the brand opens Kennels: hers, with the switch when she runs more than one, and the outside kennels on record.

Adding and changing a dog

Add dog asks first what kind of dog it is (a puppy she bred, her breeding dog, an outside dog, a pet or retired dog), which puts the right questions first: a puppy starts from its litter, which fills in the sire, dam and birth date. Papers and ownership open on request. A call name she already uses gets a warning with a way to that dog, never a block. Edit (on Profile) is the same questions, all open, plus status, the puppy's plan, ownership, what shows online, and Archive at the bottom. Add event starts with what it is: the four she adds most, then the rest by kind; types that don't fit the dog are left out (no heat cycle for a male). Each type asks its own questions, a cost becomes an expense, and Remind me puts it in Reminders (a vaccination offers "when it's due again"). Health tests go through Log a test: what's still to do first, then her usual tests; the name says how it's filed, the result is chips plus her own, and Overall (Normal or Abnormal) decides what families see. The Pedigree shows 3, 4 or 5 generations; tapping a dog shows its own, and + on a gap adds the missing parent. A Dog card shows who it went to, exactly what travels and what never does, and sends it again.

Adding litters, pairings and expenses

Add litter starts from the pairings that don't have a litter yet (or "Not planned here", with the parents and a quick pair check). Not born yet asks for the breeding date or the due date, each worked out from the other (63 days); born asks for the whelping date, how many were born alive and lost, and the delivery, with each pup recorded in Births afterwards. Her usual prices apply unless she sets this litter's own (now or later, on the litter's Pricing: male, female, deposit and full registration, each "your usual" until she picks another), and it's offered to her waitlist unless she says not this one. Saving opens the new litter, with its pairing moved along. Add pairing (on Litters › Pairings, and "Plan this pairing" on a pair check) shows the pair check's warnings as soon as both dogs are picked, then where it is: planned (her next heat offered as the date), bred (the dates, how, and the due date it gives) or confirmed. Add expense (from a litter's or dog's Expenses) takes the amount, what it was for, a category and who was paid, each with a + for her own, and what it counts toward; picking more than one shares it.

A family on the waitlist

Everything about a family hangs off their one page (One person, one page). Its tiles open one job each, and none of them asks for or repeats their name, email or phone: where a screen uses one (sign-in codes, an email she sends), it shows it with Change, which opens Their details. Offers & passes opens on their place (overall, and among families who want what they want) and passes used, then every turn they've had with its outcome and reason, then what she can do: offer a turn out of order, sell them a pup outside the turn order, move their place, pause it, or take them off the list (they withdrew, or she's removing them; 7 days to undo). Fee says what it is and where it stands (waived by a program, due, paid and counted toward the price) and marks it received, with how they paid. Messages is one thread: what they wrote on their status page, what she wrote, and each email sent, with Send again on one that failed; she writes from a starting point or her own words, by email or copied for a text. Status page shows what they see right now, when they last looked, where their sign-in codes go, what they can do there, their link, and a new link if it was shared by mistake. History is all of it, newest first.

Add family asks who first and checks as she types: an email, phone or name she already has brings up "Is this the Okafor family?", and Yes adds that person back to the list (their earlier puppy stays on their page), so nobody is ever entered twice. Then what they want, which litters (any, or only some dams and sires), a program, and whether the fee is in, with the place they'll get. A Side sale (from the turn order or a family's Offers & passes) is her pick for a family, outside the turn order: each mismatch is said plainly (a pup on hold, a different sex, another family holding that pup in their turn) and none blocks it. The price and deposit fill in from the pup's litter (or her usual) and she can change either, with the deposit due date, go-home, which payment schedule follows and notes: what the app fills in is always a starting point. Programs are hers to name; each is the same question rows as Setup (fee, place in line, time to answer, whether passes count, pausing), what families see if she writes it, and the families in it.

Add-ons

Everything a family pays for beyond the puppy is an add-on: transport and boarding after go-home sit in the same list as her own (photo session, training package, starter kit), though they're kept apart behind the scenes. Business › Add-ons holds the defaults: price, how it's charged (flat, per day, per mile), a note, and whether it's offered on new sales; Setup's transport and boarding rows open the same screen. On a sale, Add-ons is the one screen for every cost outside the puppy's price: each add-on with its default price to change for this sale, days for boarding, a one-time charge, and the puppy, add-ons, total and paid at the top. Side sale offers them as it's recorded.

Her add-ons sit in groups (Photo packages, Training), each with its own settings that its add-ons follow unless one sets its own: the link and link text under the group on a family's status page, the ages families can request and cancel them, when they're paid (with the balance, or a payment plan by percent at an age, on approval or before pickup), and whether they're paid on their own dates or with the sale's schedule. Each add-on also has a description families see, whether families can request it on their status page, and boarding days included. Transport and boarding sit in a fixed Go-home group so they read as add-ons; it can't be renamed, given settings or take other add-ons, and their prices are the Setup › Go-home answers. Pricing and schedules lists each group's and add-on's payment rule under Add-on payments, opening the same group or add-on screen.

Transport is priced per mile from her standard pickup place: fee per mile, round trip or one way, no charge under a distance, offered only to families living at least so far away (City / state becomes required on applications), and "in touch" past a limit. Check a distance gets driving miles through Mapbox (cloud backup) or Open in Maps and shows the working. Handled by sets the default for new sales: the kennel, or a third party, one of her transport partners or unknown. Each partner (a Transport account) carries a referral reward, per booking or a percent of their price; the Transport screen totals expected and received rewards by partner. On a sale, transport is either the kennel's (miles, Get driving miles, a fee prefilled from her rate) or a third party's (partner, anything charged on the sale, the reward expected or received). Pickup places (Setup › Go-home) holds her places, which one mileage starts from, which families see, and how late families can change a pickup.

Price rules (Pricing and schedules) each look at the parents or the pup, then at a name, breed, registry, title, import or trait scores (name, title and import are parents only). A pup breed price replaces the usual price; parent rules add or subtract and set the litter's price; pup rules (its puppy scores, its registry) adjust each pup's own price when it's sold. A litter's Pricing shows the working and each pup's adjustment; its own price, and every sale already made, keep theirs.

Saved schedules open their own screen: each due date's when (an age, a date, days before pickup, at pickup), what it covers, and how much (a percent of the total or of the rest, or an amount any money or only new payments can cover); the final payment is always the rest. Pricing and schedules also holds payment instructions and how the final payment is paid. A litter's Pricing can split deposit and full registration by sex and holds its own dates (deposits open, picks open time, ready to go home). A sale's Edit opens Sale details: status, date, registration, price, deposit and how it was paid, the balance (a schedule or one due date) and how it was paid, the channel and its processing fee, lead source and referrer, and why it ended, with any refund. Boarding on a sale is charged per day, week or month.

One job per screen

Long pages become a short hub: who or what it is, what needs attention, and one tile per job. Tiles that don't apply are hidden. The Dog page's 13 stacked sections become 10 tiles; the Litter page's 6 become 8, with Go-home and Weigh puppies as new single-job screens.

One person, one page

A person is one page, however she reaches them: from the Waitlist, People, a sale or search. Their name, email, phone and address show once, at the top, and Edit there changes them everywhere: offers, fee requests, sign-in codes, contracts, invoices. Their application, place in line, offers, sale and messages are tiles on that same page. No screen shows a second copy of their details, says where anything is kept, or sends her somewhere else to change what she's looking at. What a family typed on their application stays with their answers, without their name, email or phone repeated.

Waitlist

The Waitlist tab answers "what do I do next?" first. Needs you lists the open offer, new applications, fees waiting and family requests, and the tab shows that count as a badge. Selling now shows the litter with picks open, its turn order, and which pups are still open. Each item opens a screen that does that one thing: record an offer's answer, approve an application, mark a fee received, answer a request. Recording an answer moves the turn along the way the list rules already do (a pass offers the next eligible family).

Filters

Every list of records has the same filter button: a date range (presets and custom), then the fields that list has, such as status, type, category, dog, litter or family. Active filters show as chips you can remove one at a time, with a count of what's shown. Short, fixed choices (status, sex, kind) are chips; the date range is a dropdown, and so is any list that grows with her records (dams, sires, families, categories, event types, who was paid), where she ticks as many as she likes, with a find box on long lists and a count beside each. Litters are filtered by Dam and Sire separately, never by litter name: on Dogs, Litters and Sales. Built here on Dogs, a dog's History, Litters, the Waitlist list, Sales, People and Financials › Entries.

Setup (settings)

Settings get one home, More › Setup, also linked from the Home header, the desktop rail, and a "Settings for this" link on the hubs they affect (the Waitlist hub links to Waitlist settings). It opens on anything left to finish, then one row per area with a plain summary of the current answers, so she can check them without opening anything. Each area is a short list of questions with the answer beside it; tapping one shows the choices. The only switches are in What you use, which turns whole features on or off and hides their tabs, tiles and rows everywhere. Each area names where its settings live today. The same questions, in order, can be the first-run setup.

What the build would change

Pages split into hubs and section screens (for example a dog's hub plus its section pages, or one page per hub with the section in the address). Settings would move from the Kennel page cards, settings.html and inline boxes into setup pages over one settings model: program-wide answers on the kennel record (they sync), device-only ones in settings.js. nav.js would draw the tab bar and rail, a shared filter sheet would replace each list's own controls, and the waitlist would get its own hub page.

Program types

From docs/KennelOS_Program_Types_Plan.md. In Setup › How you breed she picks what her program mostly does (show / preservation, family pet, doodle or mixed-breed, working / performance, stud services, large or licensed kennel) and up to two others. That sets her tabs and their order, Home's six tiles, what's turned on in What you use, and the order of each dog's sections; her own switches win after. "View as" in the bar above switches the sample program between types. Line tools are on for show, working and stud programs and off for family pet, doodle and large kennels; pairing warnings and Relatives are never off. A stud program gets a Studs tab instead of Waitlist.

Line management

Drawn from docs/KennelOS_Line_Management_Plan.md (a plan; nothing built). Every dog is related to every other through the sire and dam links: a dog's Relatives, a pair check for any two dogs (relationship by name, COI, shared ancestors, how much pedigree is known, traits side by side, possible colors, DNA), and Litters › Lines (COI if bred for her males × her females, what each dog carries, what the program is missing, how lines produce). Planned pairings over her warning line show on Home, the pairing and both dogs. Traits are their own records, a long-lived grade per dog, with a puppy score kept beside the adult grade. Colors come from her own list as a dropdown, asked for each pup at birth; birth records include stillborn pups. What a dog carries is worked out each time from tests, her color links, its parents and its litters. The sample program is Labradors, so the color genes are real: Ash was never color tested, but yellow and chocolate pups show he carries both. The COI figures are calculated live from the sample pedigree.

Add-ons (photo sessions, training packages, kits) moved out of Setup to Business, next to Accounts, with transport and boarding after go-home listed beside them: one screen for every default cost beyond the puppy, and on each sale one Add-ons screen for that sale's. Her usual price (including male and female prices and the full registration surcharge), deposit, when payments are due, the ways families pay, and her saved payment schedules are together on one screen, Business › Pricing and schedules. They're settings, but ones she changes often, and to her a payment schedule means all of that together. Setup's row for them opens that same screen.

Every answer that's an amount, a time or a list she'd name herself ends in a +: she types her own, and it becomes one of her choices from then on. The choices shown are only common starting points. A question whose answers are fixed (yes or no, same or different) keeps them.

Lite edition

Lite shows fewer tabs and tiles through the same lists in editionConfig, so the shared core stays edition-agnostic. What you use shows Lite's Pro-only features as locked rather than hiding them.
